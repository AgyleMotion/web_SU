About us

Who we are

We are the graduate workers, teaching and research assistants, postdoctoral researchers, and academic staff who keep Stevens running: teaching the classes, running the labs, and producing the research the university is known for.

Right now, decisions about our pay, healthcare, workloads, and protections are made for us, not with us. When we form a union, those decisions become something we negotiate, together, as equals, in a contract the university is legally bound to honor. That's the difference between a promise and a guarantee.

This is about partnership, not conflict. The first step is simple: a majority of us sign union cards, and we ask Stevens to recognize our shared voice.

  • What a union is All of us, organized, bargaining one contract together, instead of negotiating alone.
  • Who it's for Grad workers, TAs, RAs, postdocs, and research staff across every department.
  • How it happens Most of us sign cards → we ask for recognition or hold an election → we negotiate a contract together.
  • Who runs it We do. Workers make every decision democratically, from the bottom up.

Why we're organizing

What we're working toward

These are the things that matter most to us, and a contract lets us put them in writing, together.

💵

Fair, livable pay

Stipends and wages that keep pace with the cost of living in the area, with raises we can plan around.

➕

Real healthcare & benefits

Affordable, comprehensive health, dental, and mental-health coverage, plus fee relief and family support.

🛡️

Job security

Clear appointments, transparent workloads, and fair notice when things change.

🌍

Support for international colleagues

Extra support for colleagues whose visas depend on their jobs, so status never has to be a worry at work.

🔬

Stable research funding

Predictable, guaranteed funding so a gap between grants doesn't derail a degree or a career.

⚖️

Respect & a fair process

A clear, fair way to raise concerns, and real protections against harassment and discrimination.

International students

A community from around the world

International researchers are at the heart of academic work at Stevens, and they carry extra worries their domestic colleagues rarely see. A union contract is one of the most practical ways to support them.

Peace of mind for visa holders

When a visa is tied to a job, uncertainty at work can feel much heavier. Contract language (clear expectations, notice periods, and a neutral process for concerns) replaces that worry with something steady you can rely on.

Funding you can plan a life around

International workers often can't take outside jobs to cover a funding gap. We're organizing for guaranteed, multi-year funding commitments, protected stipends during grant transitions, and support for the fees and costs like SEVIS, visa renewals, and travel that fall hardest on international colleagues.

Support that travels with you

From summer funding to emergency hardship funds to clear policies on remote work and travel, a contract turns "talk to your advisor" into enforceable rights that don't depend on who your PI is or where you're from.

FAQ

Questions, answered

What exactly is a union authorization card?

It's a confidential statement that you want to be represented by SAW. When a strong majority of workers sign, we can ask the university to recognize our union voluntarily or petition for an election. Signing is the most helpful step you can take.

Is it confidential? Will my advisor or PI find out?

Yes, it's confidential. Cards are seen only by the worker-led organizing committee, never by the university, your department, or your advisor. We ask you to use a personal email so nothing routes through university systems.

Does signing cost anything? Do I have to pay dues now?

No. Signing a card costs nothing and does not obligate you to pay dues. Dues only ever come up after we've formed our union and democratically voted to ratify a contract, and members decide that together.

I'm an international worker on a visa. Is it safe for me to join?

Yes. Your legally protected right to organize does not depend on immigration status, and your card is confidential. In fact, a union contract is one of the strongest supports international workers can have, because it guarantees clear, fair procedures that don't depend on your immigration status. See the International students section.

What can a union actually change?

Almost anything about our working conditions: pay, healthcare, funding guarantees, workloads, time off, grievance and anti-harassment procedures, and protections for international workers. Instead of policies handed down to us, we negotiate a binding contract.

Isn't my stipend/funding already fixed by the university?

Current levels are set by university policy, which can change over time. A contract makes funding levels and protections written commitments we agree on together, so you can plan around them.

Who is eligible to join?

Graduate workers, TAs, RAs, postdocs, and research/academic staff. If you're unsure whether you're included, reach out, and we'll help you figure it out.

How can I do more than sign?

Join the organizing committee, talk to coworkers in your department, and help collect cards. Tell us you're in via the Get involved section.
